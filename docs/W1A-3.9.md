# W1A voiceband modes (3.9)

Three receive-only modes, families 800 - 802 (engine standards 802 - 804, receivers 801 - 803, poll slots 801 - 803). The code is in
`core/src/w1a`, `app/w1a` and `tests/w1a` (ctest `w1a_dsp`). One receiver class (`W1aReceiver`) runs the three kinds: the channel is
mixed to 0 Hz (`marine::Front`) and brought to a 12 kHz complex stream the decoders read.

**Simulated signals only.** Every check ran on the generators in `core/src/w1a`. No real recording of any of these signals has been through the
decoders, so each mode still needs a test with a real recording.

| Family | Mode | Scope | Input |
|---|---|---|---|
| 800 | HF packet 300 Bd | full decode of AX.25 frames | USB audio, tones 1600 / 1800 Hz |
| 801 | Voice inversion scrambler | detection of full-band inversion, carrier estimate, descrambled audio to a WAV file | narrow-FM voice channel |
| 802 | ELT 121.5 MHz | detection only: the swept tone, its rate, range and depth | AM channel |

## Sources (checked)

* **HF packet 300 Bd.** AX.25 link layer (TAPR AX.25 v2.2: NRZI, bit stuffing, flag 0x7E, CRC-16 CCITT) and the usual HF packet physical layer:
  Bell-103-style 300 Bd AFSK with a 200 Hz shift, here the tone pair 1600 / 1800 Hz that the Dire Wolf manual gives as its 300 bit/s default
  (checked against the `direwolf` and `gen_packets` man pages; sources disagree on which tone is mark, which does not matter with NRZI). The
  receiver is the repo's own `ham::AfskRx` + `ax25::HdlcRx` (core/src/ham/ham_aprs.cpp), the one the APRS HF side already uses; nothing was copied from
  Dire Wolf (GPL-2+ source, protocol reference only; already listed in THIRD_PARTY.md).
* **Voice inversion.** Frequency inversion (f to fc - f: a mixer with a carrier fc and a low-pass) is a standard, openly described analog privacy
  technique. The speech template follows the published long-term average speech spectra (Byrne et al. 1994, "An international comparison of
  long-term average speech spectra", JASA 96(4); ITU-T P.50): a peak near 250 - 500 Hz, about -7 dB per octave above, a 300 Hz high-pass edge, a 3 kHz
  low-pass edge. No code was used.
* **ELT.** UK CAA ELT specification text and ICAO Annex 10 Vol III (as reproduced by national authorities; the ICAO text itself was not fetched):
  AM carrier, modulation factor at least 0.85, modulating tone swept DOWNWARDS over at least 700 Hz inside 1600 - 300 Hz, 2 - 4 sweeps per second.
  The duty cycle is given as 33 - 66 % by one source and 33 - 55 % by another; the detector does not depend on it (tested 35 to 100 %).

No encryption is attacked, no codec is shipped, nothing is transmitted.

## What each one reads

**HF packet 300 Bd.** Every frame with a good CRC becomes one line `FROM>TO,PATH:info [type]` in the text box; frames that fail the CRC are only counted.
Stations heard are logged as events. A tuning error of up to a quarter of the shift is followed by the AFSK receiver.

**Voice inversion scrambler.** Frames of 20 ms are analysed on a 50 Hz grid; voiced frames (above a noise floor that is tracked) are accumulated,
the noise spectrum measured in the pauses is taken off. Two fits against the speech template decide:
the *shape fit* (450 - 2700 Hz, inside the speech band) as heard and flipped about each candidate carrier 2.7 - 3.7 kHz (it separates heard from
inverted even at fc = 3.3 kHz, which maps 300 - 3000 Hz onto itself, by the spectral tilt); then the *edge fit* and the band's top edge place fc.
When the inverted fit wins by a clear margin twice in a row (and the fit is above 0.45), the carrier is reported and the audio is inverted back
with that carrier (the same mixer and a low-pass at min(fc, 3.4 kHz)). Advanced: set the carrier by hand; write the descrambled audio to a 12 kHz
16-bit mono WAV in the recordings folder (`descrambled/`). The audio is not played (the Listen path is not changed).
Limits, measured on the generator: carriers 2.8 - 3.5 kHz found within 200 Hz (usually 30 Hz at 3.0 and 3.3 kHz); the carrier estimate assumes the
original speech band starts near 300 Hz, so a source with another low edge moves it; found down to about 22 dB SNR in 3 kHz (the FM
discriminator turns that into a few dB of audio SNR); at 16 dB nothing is claimed. Needs pauses in the speech (a steady noise or an unmodulated
carrier gives no verdict); an AM signal on this FM detector gives noise bursts that can mislead it, so use the AM mode there. Split-band
inversion, rolling-code and digital scramblers are not covered.

**ELT 121.5 MHz.** The AM envelope is analysed every 10 ms over 20 ms for the strongest tone between 250 and 1850 Hz (25 Hz grid, interpolated). A
run of tones that falls by at least 600 Hz from a start above 900 Hz is one sweep; four or more sweeps with a period of 0.2 - 0.6 s (spread under
20 %) confirm the signal and give the rate. Events: found, ended. Not covered: 406 MHz beacons (COSPAS-SARSAT, a different system, family
`sarsat` already exists), the beacon's identity (the old analogue beacon sends none).

## Candidate list rows not built

* 96 numbers station logger and 97 UVB-76 monitor: the milhf (HF waveform identifier) mode already reports a buzzer-style carrier (carrier plus periodic
  buzz, its rate) and a numbers-style carrier. A schedule match needs the Priyom schedules, a web source whose licence was not checked and which
  changes weekly; nothing was invented.
* 150 AWOS / ATIS audio index: the Airband mode already follows ATIS voice on several channels at once and records it.
* 218 AMPS control channel (TIA-553): the open sources found give the 10 kbit/s FSK, the 40-bit BCH(40,28) words, the 10-bit dotting, the 11-bit word sync
  (11100010010) and the busy-idle bit every ten bits, but not the generator polynomial, the Manchester convention or the message fields in a
  checkable form; the TIA-553 text is not freely available. A "framing" mode would have had to guess, so it was skipped.
* 273 UK "Wailing" siren detect: no specification of such a signal could be found, so nothing was built.
* 98 / 219 are one mode here (family 801).
* 95 (HF packet 300 Bd) is not a second APRS mode: APRS stays one entry (family 24); this entry is the plain AX.25 300 Bd monitor.

## Tests (`ctest -R w1a_dsp`, a few seconds)

HF packet: four frames clean; eight frames with tones off by -40, 0, +35 Hz, with noise and +300 ppm; SNR 18 / 12 / 8 dB; no frame from an ELT, from
speech or from noise. Inversion: carriers 2800, 3000, 3300, 3500 Hz with a 120 Hz carrier offset and noise; the descrambled audio matches the
original long-term spectrum (correlation above 0.7) where the inverted audio does not; clear speech is called clear; noise sweep 28 / 22 / 16 dB;
a forced carrier gives audio that matches the original waveform; a bare carrier and an AFSK burst give no detection. ELT: 3 sweeps/s clean;
2, 2.5, 3.5, 4 sweeps/s with a 800 Hz offset, noise and +100 ppm; duty 35 / 50 / 66 / 100 %; SNR 20 / 10 / 5 dB (the last is not claimed);
an upward sweep, a steady tone, speech, an AFSK burst and a plain carrier in noise are not reported. The receiver class is run through the
engine's synthetic signal for all three kinds at 250 kHz with a 20 kHz dial offset.
