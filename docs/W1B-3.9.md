# Ham science event monitors (3.9, branch feat39-w1b)

Six new modes in the ham science family (`core/src/hamsci`, `app/hamsci`, one receiver class, `HamsciReceiver` kinds 10 to 15). Each has its
own mode-list entry. All are receive only. **Every signal used to test them is simulated; no recording of a real event was available.**

| mode | family / active / engine std | kind | input |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schumann resonance monitor | 820 / 821 / 822 | `kHsSchumann` | ELF (sound card or ADC), 200 Hz and up |
| Whistler and tweek detector | 823 / 824 / 825 | `kHsWhistler` | VLF audio, 16 kHz and up |
| Moon echo (EME) test | 826 / 827 / 828 | `kHsEme` | narrow RF capture, 8 ksps and up |
| Radio Jove | 829 / 830 / 831 | `kHsJove` | HF capture around 20.1 MHz, 250 ksps and up |
| e-CALLISTO solar bursts | 832 / 833 / 834 | `kHsCallisto` | one capture of the radio, 250 ksps and up |
| Aurora / sporadic-E / TEP beacons | 835 / 836 / 837 | `kHsAurora` | narrow RF capture, 8 ksps and up |

Poll slot = active standard. The families 820 to 835 are the block assigned to this branch; nothing else uses them on integ-39 (checked with
`tests/app/test_mode_numbers.cpp` rules and `docs/MODE-NUMBERS.md`).

## Skipped, and why

- **GRAVES radar (row 269)**: already in OnAir. The *Meteor scatter* mode (family 104, `core/src/mscatter`) follows the 143.050 MHz GRAVES carrier
  and logs echoes, and `mscatter_live.cpp` already matches satellite echoes in the bistatic geometry (docs/mscatter.md, docs/SAT-MISC-3.8.md).
- **Rows 138 and 270 (EME echo test / Moon echo)** are one mode: *Moon echo (EME) test*.
- Riometer, SuperSID, meteor scatter and hydrogen-line radio astronomy exist (`hamsci` kinds 6, 7; `mscatter`; `astro`) and are not duplicated.
  The new Radio Jove and CALLISTO modes are different instruments (broadband burst detection), and reuse nothing but the shared FFT helper.

## What each mode does and what it is based on

Sources were read for this work (2026-11-11) and are the only facts used. Anything not in a source is marked **ours**.

### Schumann resonance monitor
- Resonances at 7.83, 14.1, 20.3, 26.3 and 32.5 Hz, spectral width about 20 percent, modes about 6.5 Hz apart: Wikipedia, "Schumann resonances"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann_resonances). The daily maxima near 9, 14 and 20 UT (Asia, Africa, America) from the same page are
  the reason for the *trend over the day*; the monitor measures them, it does not predict them.
- Signal chain (ours): real part of the input, integrate-and-dump decimation to about 200 Hz, 2048-point Hann spectra (0.098 Hz) every 5 s,
  an average with a five minute memory, a baseline by the median over +-5 Hz. A peak is accepted when it stands 2 dB over the baseline within
  +-1.6 Hz of a published frequency; the frequency is refined by a parabola in the log domain, Q is f divided by the width at half height.
  A peak table, the average spectrum, a spectrogram of 3 to 45 Hz and the fundamental per minute are shown.
- ELF transients (ours): 5 to 40 Hz band energy in 0.5 s blocks against the median of the last five minutes (default 8 dB). They are listed as
  *transient*; they are not claimed to be Q-bursts.
- Needs an ELF sensor (induction coil, sound card). A radio cannot hear 8 Hz. 50 Hz mains stays outside the peak windows.

### Whistler and tweek detector
- Whistler frequency range about 1 to 30 kHz, usually peaking at 3 to 5 kHz, tone falling over seconds (Wikipedia, "Whistler (radio)"
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistler_(radio)); the dispersion law t = D / sqrt(f) (Toyokawa et al., as quoted in the search result for
  "whistler dispersion"; the page itself gives no formula).
- Tweek: waveguide cut-off near 1.6 to 1.8 kHz with harmonics at multiples (1.8 kHz and 3.6 kHz in the cited Japanese studies, 1.6 kHz and 1.75 kHz
  fitted in another; maximum harmonic seen six): Annales Geophysicae 26, 1451 (2008) and the other papers returned by the search. The OnAir
  detector uses n x 1.8 kHz as the centre of a +-0.4 kHz acceptance window, so 1.6 kHz works too (ours).
- Detector (ours): 512-point spectra every 5.3 ms, a per-bin noise floor (20 s memory). *Tweek*: after a broadband impulse, a ridge in the next
  16 to 48 ms that is 6 dB above the same rows 600 Hz away, quiet above 6 kHz, in a mode-1 or mode-2 window and not rising with time.
  *Whistler*: a path sum over t = t0 + D / sqrt(f) for 1.5 to 9 kHz, D from 10 to 300 s sqrt(Hz) in 7 % steps (**ours**: the D range is a search
  range, not a published limit), accepted at a mean of 6 dB (clipped to 15 dB) with at least 75 % of the path points over 4 dB. Real whistlers
  follow the law only well below the nose frequency; the path model is a first approximation.
- Output: spectrogram 0.5 to 10 kHz, event list, events per minute, sferics per minute, events per UTC hour.

### Moon echo (EME) test
- Doppler shift up to about +-440 Hz and a spread of up to about 4 Hz at 144 MHz from libration; coherence bandwidth a few kHz; the WSJT lunar-echo
  tests report delays of about 2.6 s: Bob Atkins, "EME Echo Doppler Spreading and Lunar Libration" (https://bobatkins.com/radio/doppler_broadining_libration.html),
  K1JT, "Lunar Echoes" QEX (https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/LunarEchoes_QEX.pdf), dxmaps.com "EME libration fading".
- The search window 2.3 to 2.85 s after the start of your own pulse is ours, chosen to cover the Earth-Moon distance range with margin.
- Method (ours): find the strong keyed carrier (25 dB over the noise in a 3.9 Hz bin, at least 0.5 s), then for each pulse score a three-bin,
  pulse-length window at every delay and Doppler bin in the echo window and compare against the same search at delays after the echo window
  (from 3.15 s plus the pulse length, 0.6 s wide; default 6 sigma). Reported: delay (parabolic interpolation), Doppler against the carrier, level over the noise in
  12 Hz and below your carrier, spread at half height. Pulses need to be about 10 s apart (windows that overlap another pulse are skipped).
- The transmitter is yours; nothing is keyed or transmitted by OnAir.

### Radio Jove
- NASA Radio Jove: 20.1 MHz inside the Jupiter decametric band, above the ionospheric cut-off; the kit receiver is narrow band; it makes no
  difference whether one listens at 20.10 or 20.150 MHz for Jupiter or the Sun; Jupiter storms do not occur above about 40 MHz
  (https://radiojove.gsfc.nasa.gov/faq, https://radiojove.gsfc.nasa.gov/library/pubs/docs/RadioJoveSummary2014.pdf, search results of 2026-11-11;
  the project home page's certificate was expired when fetched).
- Method (ours): FFT of the whole capture (about 31 kHz bins, 0.1 s rows), per-bin background with its own spread that follows quiet rows
  (20 s memory), a burst is a run of rows with half of the bins at least 1 dB (or 5 sigma) over the background. Narrowband interference does not
  reach half the bins and is ignored. Start, duration, peak, width, short or long (under or over 1 s); the background level per minute follows
  the galactic noise (and any gain drift).
- Burst types of Jupiter (L and S bursts) are not classified: 0.1 s rows cannot resolve S-bursts.

### e-CALLISTO solar bursts
- e-CALLISTO: 45 to 870 MHz, about 300 kHz radiometric bandwidth, 62.5 kHz frequency resolution, 200 channels per sweep, 0.25 s time resolution,
  data as FITS files uploaded to FHNW (https://www.e-callisto.org/). Files are named station_date_time_code.fit.gz and hold 15 minute frames
  (SPASE record on barionleg.github.io/hpde.io, arXiv 2603.26086).
- This mode is **not** a CALLISTO spectrometer: one capture covers a few MHz, there is no frequency sweep, and no FITS file is written or read.
  It uses the published bin width (62.5 kHz where the capture allows it, at least eight bins) and row time (0.25 s), a spectrogram, and a burst list with
  the start, duration, strength, bandwidth and a **measured** drift rate in MHz per second (least-squares line through the per-row centroid of
  the bins over the background). A burst is called *drifting* when the drift moves it by two bins or more. The burst types I to V are not named:
  the classification thresholds are not in the sources used.

### Aurora / sporadic-E / TEP beacons
- No source was found for a numeric split between these propagation types, so the classes are by signal character only and say so in the event
  text (**ours**): Doppler width of 40 Hz or more is called aurora-like, a carrier that fades by 3 dB or more (standard deviation) is called
  TEP or scatter-like, a steady carrier is called sporadic-E-like. The path is not proven by any of them.
- Method (ours): 2 kHz wide window, 1024-point spectra every 0.51 s, a scan statistic over sliding windows of 5, 13, 31, 77 and 155 bins
  (z over the median noise, 8 by default), the narrowest window within 85 % of the best z gives the width; an opening needs four rows in a row
  and ends after ten seconds without. Output: start, duration, peak S/N, width, shift, flutter, a spectrogram, peak S/N per minute and events per hour.

## Exports
All six: **Copy events (CSV)**, **Copy trend (CSV)** and **Save CSV files** (`<data folder>/<id>_events.csv`, `<id>_trend.csv`). Event columns:
`utc, signal_time_s, type, frequency_hz, peak_db, duration_s, aux1, aux2, note` (aux1 and aux2: whistler D and 0, tweek mode, echo delay and spread, burst
drift and bandwidth, opening width and flutter). Android and the web panel get the generic cards and tables from the telemetry structs
(`gen_results_map.py`, `gen_web_schema.py`).

## Tests (all simulated)
`tests/hamsci/test_hamsci_evt.cpp` (ctest `hamsci_evt`) and `test_hamsci_evt_rx.cpp` (`hamsci_evt_rx`), with the existing `hamsci_mode` wiring test.
Impairments: sound-card frequency error (+0.4 Hz) and drift, noise sweep, 50 Hz mains, keyed narrowband interference, a 1 dB gain drift, carrier drift
of the transmitter, Doppler spread, amplitude flutter, 24 and 48 kHz input rates, noise-only and no-echo controls. Longer noise sweeps run with
`ONAIR_TEST_LONG=1`.

## Not validated
- No real recording of a Schumann resonance, whistler, tweek, Jovian or solar burst, Moon echo or beacon opening was used; the sensitivities and
  false-alarm rates measured in the tests hold for the simulated noise only (white, Gaussian), not for real receivers with interference, hum and gain drift.
- The simulated whistlers follow the law the detector searches for, and the simulated tweeks follow the waveguide group-delay formula with a
  10 ms path constant; real ones differ.
