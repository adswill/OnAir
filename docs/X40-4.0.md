# 4.0 signal modes: RC PPM, UVB-76, GRAVES, Radio Jove, beacon monitor

Five receive-only modes that share one receiver (`core/src/x40`, screens in `app/x40`, tests in `tests/x40`). Families 881 to 885, see `docs/FAMILY-NUMBERS-4.0.md`.
**All five were checked on simulated signals only.** No real recording was available. The generators live in `tests/x40/x40_gen.h` and are not reachable from the application.
None of these has a published bit-level specification to decode against: they measure a signal. The mode text says so.

## RC model PPM (family 881)
Finds the pulses of an AM-keyed or FM-shifted PPM radio-control transmitter. The pulse is the state the carrier spends less time in, so both polarities work. The time from one pulse
to the next is a channel (accepted 0.55 to 2.7 ms), a gap above 3.5 ms is the sync that closes a frame. Shows channel count, frame length and every channel in ms and in percent of travel.
- Tested: 2 to 9 channels, both polarities, carrier offsets 0 / +12 / -15 kHz, clock error 0 / +5 % / -3 %, timing jitter 8 us, noise sweep, a drifting carrier.
- Result (carrier to noise in 40 kHz): 30, 20 and 14 dB all give 87 of 100 frames of a 2 s run; 10 dB gives 8. Noise alone, a steady carrier and a slowly amplitude-modulated carrier give no frame.
- Not covered: PCM and spread-spectrum (2.4 GHz) systems, per-model channel names, servo-reversal.

## UVB-76 buzzer monitor (family 882)
Times a 4625 kHz AM signal: the audio energy (carrier removed, about 1.5 kHz of audio) is gated into buzz events; shows buzz rate per minute, buzz length, period and its spread, logs
a break in the train and any transmission much longer than a buzz. **No voice decoding, no message meaning.** The reference numbers (about 25 buzzes a minute) are from public descriptions; the mode measures what it hears.
- Tested: periods 2.0 / 2.4 / 3.0 s, carrier offset 0 / +700 / -2500 Hz with 3 Hz/s drift, carrier-to-noise 40 / 25 / 15 dB in the audio band, period drift 2 %. Rate within 0.6 per minute and length within 0.12 s in all cases.
  A break of 20 s and a 9 s long transmission are each logged once. Noise alone and a plain carrier log nothing (60 s each).
- Limits: the carrier must lie within about 4.5 kHz of the tuned frequency; below 15 dB in the audio band it does not lock.

## GRAVES meteor echoes (family 883)
The GRAVES radar sends an unmodulated carrier on 143.050 MHz. The carrier is the strongest steady line of the averaged spectrum (1024-point Blackman-Harris at 2.4 kHz, 2.3 Hz bins, 50 % overlap) and follows the radio's error;
an echo is a peak outside the carrier skirt (5 bins) more than 12 dB over the noise in at least two spectra, tracked within 4 bins. Row: Doppler, strength, duration, drift.
- Tested: carrier at 0 / +150 / -380 Hz, echo Doppler -700 to +420 Hz, two echoes per run, Doppler measured within 12 Hz, carrier measured within 4 Hz. 10 echoes per run against strength over the noise in a bin:
  24, 20, 17 dB: 10 of 10; 14 dB: 8 of 10; 10 dB: 0 of 10. 10 minutes with the carrier and noise: 0 false echoes; 2 minutes of noise only: nothing.
- The threshold (12 dB) is set so that noise alone is expected to give about one false echo in some hundred hours; lowering it gains about 2 dB of sensitivity at that cost.

## Radio Jove bursts (family 884)
Band power (5 kHz) in 5 ms blocks, a baseline and scatter that follow the quiet level, a burst is 3 or more blocks above the baseline by 4 scatter widths. At most 100 ms is a short burst, longer is long.
It cannot resolve the millisecond structure of real S-bursts and cannot tell interference from Jupiter or the Sun.
- Tested: 6.0 and 4.0 dB bursts of 0.06 / 0.08 s and 0.5 / 3 s are all found with the right class; 2.6 dB bursts are not reliable (fragmented). A 3 dB baseline drift does not make a burst. 30 minutes of noise: 0 false bursts.

## Beacon propagation monitor (family 885)
Follows one steady beacon (28 / 50 / 144 / 432 MHz) in 2048-point spectra at 2.4 kHz (1.17 Hz bins) and measures the level over the noise, the Doppler spread of the line (second moment of its own region of the 9-bin-smoothed spectrum) and the swing of the level over 5 spectra (about 4 s).
Classes: steady, fading (swing above 6 dB), aurora-like (spread above 40 Hz; a steady line reads about 8 Hz), none (under 10 dB). A change of class held for two decisions is logged. A reading of the signal, not a forecast; a keyed or drifting beacon will read as something else.
- Tested: line offsets 0 / +180 / -420 Hz, 35 and 30 dB over the noise in a bin: steady, fading (0.12 Hz fading, 14 dB) and aurora-smeared (+-120 Hz) are each classed correctly; a line drifting 4 Hz/s stays steady; a beacon that disappears and returns gives the class changes; 5 minutes of noise: no class change.
  Lower levels (for the record): at 20 dB steady and fading are right but aurora reads steady; at 15 and 12 dB steady is right, fading reads steady and aurora reads none.

## Plumbing
One `X40Receiver`, as the aviation/rail/road receiver. `Engine::x40()`, `RxTelemetry::x40`, poll slots 881 to 884, `x40Tuning(v)` for the band, rate and bandwidth. A mode-list entry each (RC PPM: IoT & Sensors, UVB-76: HF Utility, GRAVES, Jove and the beacon monitor: Weather & Science).
Generated files (MODES.md, web schema and options, the Android lists) were regenerated with the repository's generators.
