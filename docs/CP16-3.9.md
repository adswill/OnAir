# CP16 (3.9, branch feat39-cp16, GitHub request #474)

CP16 ("Chinese Pattern 16") is the picture-text mode of the Chinese Radio Amateurs Club (CRAC). A 16 x 16 dot character is scanned row by row; each of 16 audio carriers is switched on or off by one column, and any waterfall shows the character upright. OnAir has it as its own mode entry, family 880 (engine standard 882), receive and transmit.

## Sources

- CRAC's design and field-test report, 2013 ("抗干扰新汉字通信模式 CP16 设计原理及现场试验报告", crac.org.cn).
- CRAC's article on the transmission specification (cloud.tencent.com/developer/news/1907938): the worked example of the timing and the bandwidth.
- CRAC's downloads page (crac.org.cn/?p=1019) and its 40 s sample recording, measured but not included in the repository, because its licence is not stated.
- T/RAC 019-2019 (业余无线电16载频图形文字（CP16）技术要求) is the standard itself. **Its full text is not published free** (the standards index lists no download), so it could not be read. Everything below comes from the documents above.
- CP16+ by BD8DQS (MIT, github.com/hatsunesenya/cp16_plus): protocol document read, two recordings with their truth files used as test vectors. No CP16+ code is included. CP16+ says itself that it is not compatible with CP16.
- The CRAC sender (a LabVIEW program) was not downloaded or used.

## The signal

| Item | Value | Where from |
|---|---|---|
| Carriers | 16, equally spaced | CRAC |
| Spacing | 10 to 20 Hz (OnAir accepts 5 to 30) | CRAC article; the trials used 15 to 19 Hz |
| Keying | on for an inked dot, off for a blank one, equal levels | CRAC |
| Scan | one dot row per time step, top row first | CRAC article; order confirmed on the CRAC sample |
| Columns | carrier i = column i, lowest audio frequency = left column on upper sideband | confirmed on the CRAC sample (the letters read the right way round) |
| Character | 16 rows and one blank row: row = character time / 17 | CRAC article |
| Speed | 1 to 4 s a character | CRAC article |
| Text | GB2312 dot characters | CRAC |

Measured on the CRAC sample: lowest carrier 1088 Hz, spacing 19.17 Hz, rows of 224 ms (3.81 s a character), letters sent as full-width forms. Its row timing is irregular between characters: the LabVIEW program is not steady.

## Choices where the public documents say nothing

- Lowest carrier: 1000 Hz by default, any value (the CRAC sample has 1088 Hz).
- Defaults: 15 Hz and 2 s a character, inside the CRAC ranges.
- Edges: raised cosine, 6 % of a row and at most 8 ms, with every carrier's phase running on. The CRAC guide names the clicks of hard switching as a defect of its own sender.
- Space: a blank cell. ASCII: full-width forms.
- Font: an HZK16 file or a .ttf / .otf the user chooses; otherwise the bundled Noto Sans CJK SC (SIL OFL 1.1) drawn at 16 dots. HZK16 itself is not shipped: its origin and licence are unclear.

## Receiving

The carriers and the rows are measured automatically on either sideband. The screen shows the received dots as a picture, which is how CP16 is meant to be read, and also reads the characters by comparing the dots with the font. Reading is only as good as the match between the sender's font and the one in use: with the bundled font a station using HZK16 gives a clear picture but unreliable characters; choosing an HZK16 file under Advanced fixes that. Base frequency, spacing, character time and sideband can be fixed under Advanced.

No voice codec is involved, so the user-installed codec rule does not apply.

## Transmitting

The Transmit panel sends the typed text once as sideband IQ. Options: seconds a character, spacing, lowest carrier, sideband.

## Tests

`tests/cp16/test_cp16_all.cpp` (long sweeps with `ONAIR_TEST_LONG=1`). Run on GitHub (quick.yml, Linux); the Mac was on battery, so nothing was run locally on the final code.

**Simulated only**, apart from the CP16+ files, which come from the CP16+ software and were not received off the air. No off-air CP16 recording was available, and the CRAC sample is not in the repository.

Long sweep (`ONAIR_TEST_LONG=1`, every speed x spacing x sideband at 20 dB, then an SNR sweep). This is **not passing**; the results show where the receiver is limited (quick.yml run 42986856273):

- Read fully: every combination at 3 s a character; 2 s a character at 12 to 20 Hz and at 15 Hz; 1 s a character at 17 Hz.
- Wrong row timing, so little is read: 2 s at 10 and 11 Hz on one sideband (the other sideband reads); 1.5 s at 10 to 12 Hz; 1 s at 10 to 15 Hz and 19 to 20 Hz. Rows hardly longer than 1 / spacing (spacing x row time below about 1.3) blur the edges the timing is measured from. Two other row searches were tried and made the common case worse, so they were taken out.
- Partly read (71 to 95 %): 1.5 s at 15 to 20 Hz.
- 4 s a character: rows are found to within 1 %, but reading breaks down after the first few characters. Not solved.
- SNR in 2500 Hz at 2 s and 15 Hz: 10, 0 and -3 dB 100 %; -5 dB 65 %; -8 dB 15 %; -10 dB 0 %; -13 dB no rows found. Reading by eye from the picture goes lower than reading by machine; that was not measured.
- On CRAC's own sample (an earlier build, local): carriers 1088.1 Hz / 19.17 Hz and rows of 224.2 ms (3.81 s a character) were found, matching the hand measurement. The characters were not read: its font is not the bundled one.

What is left: the row search for short rows, reading at 4 s, machine reading below -3 dB, and a test on an off-air recording with an HZK16 font loaded.
