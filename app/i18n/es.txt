# COSPAS-SARSAT 406 MHz
A 406 MHz distress beacon (EPIRB on a boat, ELT in an aircraft, PLB carried by a person) sends a burst of about half a second every 50 seconds: an unmodulated carrier, then 112 or 144 bits at 400 bit/s. The message holds a 15 digit hex ID that is unique to the beacon, the country, the kind of beacon, and often a position (rounded to 15 minutes in a short message, to 4 seconds in a long one). Two BCH checks protect the message; up to three damaged bits in the first field are repaired.\n\nOnAir only receives. Test beacons and self-test bursts are marked. If you think you heard a real one, tell the rescue coordination centre of your country; do not rely on this program for safety. A satellite beacon is a carrier, sometimes phase modulated with telemetry, that ground stations use to measure the link. The carrier is followed in frequency and level; the telemetry is recovered as raw frames and the frame length is learned from their repetition. The layout of a given satellite's frames is not decoded.	Una baliza de satélite es una portadora, a veces modulada en fase con telemetría, que las estaciones terrestres usan para medir el enlace. Se sigue la portadora en frecuencia y nivel; la telemetría se recupera como tramas en bruto y la longitud de trama se aprende de su repetición. La estructura de las tramas de un satélite concreto no se decodifica.
